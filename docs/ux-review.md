# UX review: what made Murmur hard to love, and what changed

A review of the phone experience (390 x 844, Traditional Chinese), measured on the screens as they were, with what popular wellness apps and usability research suggest.

## Frictions found

1. **Today led with the voice recorder**, while 換角度 is the core. A newcomer saw a big microphone, a three-option welcome card and four small tool buttons before anything about looking at a worry.
2. **Three decisions on one screen** when naming the worry: the text, six kinds of worry, and eleven heaviness buttons. Twenty buttons on one phone screen.
3. **Fourteen theory names to choose from.** The list was 1789 px tall, and the names are textbook names.
4. **Explanations before action.** The lens home opened with a paragraph and a disclaimer; each question carried its reason underneath.
5. **The best moment was below the fold.** The keepsake card, the most delightful part, sat after the rating and three buttons, 2180 px down.
6. **Typing on a phone** for every answer, with no quick way to say "not sure".
7. **No sense of progress** in the flow, and no companion or motion to make it feel alive.

## What the research says

- **One step at a time.** Staged disclosure keeps each step's purpose clear ([NN/g, progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)).
- **Secondary actions in a bottom sheet** with a visible close button, never stacked ([NN/g, bottom sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)).
- **A companion that reacts.** Duolingo's characters react to the learner ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/building-character/)); Finch's pet grows with the person ([Finch](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37935669335309-Our-Approach-to-Self-Care)).
- **Tap before type.** How We Feel finds the feeling word through a color grid and tags ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384)).
- **A fixed primary button and a progress bar** lower friction ([Pratt design critique of Finch](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-finch-self-care-pet-ios-app/)).
- **Celebrate completion, not streaks.** Streaks work through fear of loss; one missed day can end use ([Mobbin, 859 streak designs](https://mobbin.com/blog/streaks-are-about-losing-a-study-of-859-streak-designs); [Duolingo gamification study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16175)).
- **Low return is normal** in mental health apps: median 30-day retention 3.3% ([Baumel et al., 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567)). Each session has to be worth it on its own, rather than the app chasing daily use. Low engagement is tied to poor design, low trust and weak help in emergencies ([Torous et al., 2018](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/clinical-review-of-user-engagement-with-mental-health-smartphone-/)).

## What changed

- **Mumu (姆姆)**, an original companion: a cloud-mochi with a sprout. Idle, listening while the person types, thinking while something loads, happy when something is done, sleepy at night. All motion is off when the device asks for reduced motion. Mumu never appears on the help screen and is never used to make anyone feel guilty.
- **換角度 as staged screens** with a progress bar: the worry, then the kinds of worry as emoji chips, then heaviness as a slider with weather.
- **Three suggested approaches as picture cards** in plain words, with the rest one tap away.
- **One question per screen**, the reason behind a tap, "not sure" as a chip.
- **The summary opens with the card** and a small celebration, then the person's words, the perspective and one thing to try, a rating with faces, and one Save button. Everything else is in a "More" sheet.
- **Today leads with 換角度**; the recorder and the other tools are smaller.

## What to watch (on the device only)

Completion of 換角度 and where people stop; time to the first answer; chips versus typing; cards saved or shared; the one-tap rating; whether people come back on their own within 7 and 30 days. Nothing is sent anywhere; if these counts are added, they belong on a "my data" screen the person can read and delete.
