# Results: learned featured-item policy against the expert order

Generated by `run_study.py` from the preregistered design in `PREREG.md`. Every number comes from synthetic users whose parameters are assumptions. None of it is evidence about real people.

## Checks that gate everything else

- **Safety-filter violations: 0** across 4 worlds, 6 policies, 1000 users and 90 sessions each (2,160,000 decisions). Rule 1 is met.
- **Negative control (null world):** no learned policy shows a gain interval above zero at any horizon. Rule 2 is met.
- About .23 of sessions were gated (low mood, risk, or the loss topic) and showed the expert's first item. About .62 of sessions were rated.

## Main finding (primary policy `murmur`, primary world `heterogeneous`)

**Under the primary assumptions the learned policy does not beat the expert order within 90 interactions by the preregistered rule** (rule 6: the mean gain interval above zero and most users better off, at the same horizon).

| Horizon | Mean gain (98.33% interval) | Regret vs expert | Rule 3: beats on average | Rule 4: at least .03 per session | Share of users better off (95% interval) | Share worse off | Rule 5: most users benefit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | +0.03 (-0.03 to +0.08) | -0.03 | no | no (needs +0.90) | .48 (.45 to .51) | .51 | no |
| 60 | +0.11 (-0.01 to +0.23) | -0.11 | no | no (needs +1.80) | .49 (.46 to .52) | .51 | no |
| 90 | +0.23 (+0.05 to +0.41) | -0.23 | yes | no (needs +2.70) | .50 (.47 to .53) | .50 | no |

Gain is in expected helpful sessions, summed over the horizon, learned policy minus expert order, for the same simulated user on the same days. Regret is the same number with the sign flipped.

For scale: the expert order's expected helpfulness per session in this world is .58, and a policy that knew each user's best item every day would add .14 per session (+12.92 over 90 sessions). The learned policy captured +0.23 of that. Counting realised helpful sessions instead of expected ones (what a person would actually live through), .44 of users had more helpful sessions than under the expert order at 90 and .42 had fewer, so one person could not tell the difference from their own experience.

Break-even (descriptive): the mean cumulative gain stays above zero from session 25 on.

At T = 90, about the length of one person's log, the usual IPS interval covers the truth .84 to .90 of the time and the usual doubly robust interval .88 to .92, against a nominal .95 (Monte Carlo error about .005 to .008). At T = 500 the figures are .93 to .94 and .93 to .94. The point estimates are close to unbiased; it is the intervals that are too narrow. Propensities here depend on earlier rewards, so the scores are not independent draws with one variance, which is what the usual interval assumes. This check cannot separate that from the plain small-sample problem of a few heavily weighted observations.

The adaptively weighted estimator has the smallest spread, but at T = 90 its coverage is .89 to .92, so it does not restore nominal coverage at this length either, and it carries a small downward bias. It was checked only on this non-contextual problem. **Confidence intervals from one person's adaptively collected log should not be reported.** Intervals for contextual policy values from such logs are out of scope for this module.

## What this does and does not show

- It shows how these policies behave against the expert order when users are built as `murmur_rl/env.py` builds them. The size of person-to-person differences, the drift, the follow rate and the rating model are assumptions. Different assumptions give different answers; the `strong` and `misspecified` worlds show how far.
- It does not show that personalisation helps or fails to help real people, and it does not justify turning exploration on for anyone but the owner.
- The safety result is a property of this code under these inputs. The in-app policy needs its own tests against `POLICY_SPEC.md` section 10.
